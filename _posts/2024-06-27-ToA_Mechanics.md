## Weather
Chult is hot, humid, and rainy throughout the year. The temperature regularly climbs as high as 95 degrees during the day and seldom falls below 70 degrees F even at night. A day without rain is rare, but rain varies from a steady mist to drenching downpours.

Visibility in heavy rain is limited to 50 yards. Beyond that distance, only Huge or larger objects can be distinguished. Missile weapon ranges are halved during rain.

### Adjusted Weather
Temperatures will range from the mid-60s to low-100s. This will have no bearing on the story. The weather is also randomized and there may be multiple instances of bad weather.

## Travel Distances
On the map of Chult, each hex measures 10 miles across. Characters moving at a normal pace can travel 1 hex per day on foot through coastal, jungle, mountain, swamp, or wasteland terrain. They can travel 2 hexes per day if they’re traveling by canoe on a river or lake. The rate of travel up or down river is the same; the rivers are so sluggish that current is almost imperceptible. Without canoes, the normal rate of travel along a river is the same as through the surrounding terrain. Canoes move 1 hex per day through swamp.

## Navigation
Choose a navigator to roll Survival checks. This can change daily, or be one person. At the start of each new travel day, the DM makes a Wisdom (Survival) check on behalf of the navigator. The result of the check determines whether or not the party becomes lost over the course of the day. The DC of the check is based on the day’s most common terrain: DC 10 for coasts and lakes, or DC 15 for jungles, mountains, rivers, swamps, and wastelands.

If the check succeeds, the navigator knows exactly where the party is on the players’ map of Chult throughout the day.

If the check fails, the party becomes lost. 

The next time a navigator succeeds on a Survival check made to navigate, reveal the party’s actual location to the players.

### Adjusted Navigation
Designated navigator will roll Survival check on behalf of the group. Instead of rolling daily checks, the navigator will roll twice: Survival check to see if they get lost, and a navigator's dice to see how many days they travel uninterrupted. Depending on the party, the dice can be either a 1d4, 1d6, 1d8 and so on.

If the navigator fails the Survival check, they can roll again on the next day to see if this is noticed. If not they can continue to roll each day until either successful Survival check is made, or they have expended all the days from their navigator's dice.

## Dehydration
Characters who explore Chult need plenty of water to stay hydrated. The water found in rivers and on the ground is unfit for drinking unless it is boiled first. If they have a rain catcher, characters can use it to collect rain and then store the water in portable containers.

At the end of each day, any character who hasn’t drunk at least 2 gallons of fresh water must succeed on a DC 15 Constitution saving throw or suffer 1 level of exhaustion. The saving throw is made with disadvantage if the character is wearing medium armor, heavy armor, or heavy clothing. Characters traveling at a fast pace, instead of a normal or slow pace, take a –5 penalty on their saving throws against dehydration.

### Adjusted Dehydration
While within the city of Port Nyanzaru, characters who refrain from strenuous activity can get away with 1 gallon of water per day as opposed to 2 gallons. There are stations around the port with water collection and dispensing devices at no financial cost.

Players who find a genuinely creative way to narratively cool themselves down after wearing their armor during the heat of the day may avoid rolling with penalty for exhaustion.
